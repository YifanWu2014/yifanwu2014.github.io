More about My Research

I ask how information from AI should be generated, evaluated, and used. Drawing on game theory and statistical decision theory, I model data providers and predictive models as strategic information sources, and downstream decision processes as rational decision-makers. This perspective connects the incentives that shape information with its value for decisions.

One central question is how AI predictions can support good downstream decisions. Predictive accuracy alone does not guarantee small decision loss. I develop calibration measures and algorithms that connect prediction quality to downstream decision payoffs. I also study how next-token predictors can support decisions with guarantees in adversarial environments.

A second question is how to incentivize the acquisition and reporting of high-quality information. I design scoring rules that reward strategic data providers for acquiring more informative data. For predictive models, I develop truthful calibration measures so that optimizing an evaluation objective aligns with producing higher-quality predictions. These questions connect mechanism design, prediction evaluation, and learning.

My theoretical work is complemented by empirical studies in machine learning and human-AI interaction. I develop theoretical benchmarks for understanding how people use AI-provided information and study the strategic robustness of text evaluation methods. These collaborations allow me to evaluate theoretical insights in practice, while empirical findings motivate new theoretical questions.
